In search of the unknown bird

Ochin makes stories, magazines and pictures that help people fall in love with what the world knows, and with what it still wonders about.

It is written in English with a Bengali heart, drawn by hand, and made for every age.

This page is the rulebook. Anything that makes Ochin pages, whether a person, Quire or a script, reads it and does not restate it.

The idea

ভাবনা

অচিন (Ochin) means the unknown, the one not yet met. The word comes from the best-known song of Lalon Fakir, the nineteenth-century Baul poet of Bengal: khachar bhitor ochin pakhi, an unknown bird that flies in and out of the cage.

For Lalon the bird is the soul. For Ochin it is every question nobody has fully answered yet: why the night sky is dark, how light became a photograph, what a self is. The cage is everything we already know. Each story follows one bird.

That gives the brand its one promise: we never pretend to catch the bird. We show where it flies, who chased it before, and what is still open. Wonder is the product; certainty is not.

The magazine does not hand you the bird. It shows you where it flies.Any topic on earth, seen through Bengali eyes: the way Satyajit Ray drew the whole world for Sandesh in his own hand.

Who decides what

কে কী ঠিক করে

Variety and identity used to fight, because every section was told to invent its own world. They stop fighting once each layer may only choose from the layer above it, never redefine it. Variety lives at the bottom of this list.

Brandfixed · this page

Owns: the nine colours and paper, the four typefaces and the ladder, the shape kit and its meanings, the finish, the bird, the bilingual rule, the voice.

Changes: almost never, and only by a new version of this page.

Seriesper magazine title

Owns: masthead lockup, trim and margins, grid, folio style, which signatures the series leans on.

Chooses from: the brand. It may not add a colour or a face.

Sectionper topic

Owns: two of the six section colours, one illustration register, which signature leads (kantha for a journey, alpana for a ritual, the bird for a question).

Chooses from: the brand's options. Sections still look different; they never look foreign.

Spreadper two pages

Owns: the device (a film strip across the fold, a magnifier, an exploded stack), the composition, the crop.

Must be fresh: no device twice running. This is where the surprise lives.

Type and language

The bilingual rule দুই ভাষা

English carries the reading. Bengali carries the heart. Roughly 85 words in 100 are English. A reader with no Bengali misses nothing they need; a Bengali reader finds home on every spread.

  • Always Bengali: the mark অচিন, folio numerals (১২), section names as an eyebrow beside the English one.
  • Once per story: one Bengali line, either an epigraph, a pull quote from a Bengali source, or a closing line, with its English beneath in italic.
  • The word box (শব্দ): one Bengali word per feature that names the idea better than English does, with how it sounds and what it means.
  • Never: whole paragraphs side by side. It halves the page and doubles the effort.
  • Bengali text is shaped, not typed: where a tool cannot shape Bengali (Affinity), it is drawn as outlines by HarfBuzz.

Shapes and what they mean

চিহ্ন ও অর্থ

Every drawn shape comes from one kit, made in Affinity with the Ochin Shape script, and every shape stands for something. A shape used without its meaning is decoration, and decoration is what makes a brand look like a template.

Lal paar লাল পাড়

The red border of the Puja sari. It marks an occasion and a threshold: you are entering, or you are leaving.

Use: the foot of covers, the edge of section openers, the back cover. Never inside a running page.

Matra rule মাত্রা

The headstroke Bengali letters hang from. It is what makes an English headline look Bengali-born: knowledge needs a line to hold on to.

Use: above every headline. It is furniture, so it never counts against a ration.

Kantha stitch কাঁথা

The running stitch that turns old saris into new quilts. Old pieces, joined, become something warm: that is how knowledge is made.

Use: only when it connects: a path through a diagram, a timeline, a river's route, a line from a word to what it names. Never as a border for its own sake.

Alpana আলপনা

Rice-paste drawn on the floor at a doorway to welcome what comes. Made by hand, symmetrical, gone by next week: order found in nature, offered as a greeting.

Use: to open a section and to mark the end of a story, as a lens or a frame around a picture. One per spread at most.

The bird অচিন পাখি

The mark itself: the question. It may perch, turn back, or fly, and it may be any colour. One rule never bends: the bird is never drawn inside a cage. We chase it; we do not catch it.

Use: the cover, the last line of every story (small, on its line), the back cover. At most one per spread.

Panel পট

A patch of colour under text or a picture, cut like cloth or a pat scroll. Its edge says where you are: a wave for water, a scallop for petals, a zigzag for mountains or stitching, steps for ghats and buildings.

Use: freely, with a plain edge by default. A profiled edge must match the subject. Any edge may fade to clear, or a panel may grade into a second brand colour.

Festival ornaments উৎসব

Generated alpona, kolam, rosettes and woven border bands. Rich, and therefore rare: they belong to festivals, crafts and cultures, where the pattern is the subject.

Use: only when the topic is ritual, craft or pattern. Never as a page filler. One per issue outside those topics.

Finish

বুনোট

Ochin is printed, never just rendered. Every drawn shape is finished the same way, with seeded noise worked into the material rather than a filter laid over the page. Type stays sharp. Imperfection is the point: wonder is made by hands.

WobbleEvery edge is displaced by slow noise, so nothing is ruled.

WatercolourPigment pools, the dried edge darkens, colour bleeds into the paper in a broken halo.

WearInk lifted in fine specks gathered in patches, like a block print.

GrainInside the ink only, never floated over the type.

PaperWarm shankha with a slow mottle and its own grain.

SeededEach shape has its own seed: no two alike, and any one can be rebuilt exactly.

The page

পাতা
  • Trim A4, 210 × 297 mm, 3 mm bleed. Pages are designed as spreads, never alone.
  • Margins 16 mm all round, six columns with 4 mm gutters. Pictures may bleed; text never crosses the fold.
  • Folio at the foot, outer corner, every page: Bengali numerals, then Ochin · issue title in Anek at 7.5 pt. Same place, same size, always.
  • Headline hangs from its matra rule. Eyebrow above in label caps, Bengali section name beside it.
  • Capacity is measured, not hoped: text frames are test-poured, and overflow is a design bug.
  • Pacing no device twice running; every loud spread is followed by a quiet or dense one.

Pictures

ছবি

Photographs

Real, and credited on the page or the credits page: public domain or open licence, with source and licence recorded. Crops are part of the story; a detail that matters gets its own magnifier. A photograph is never recoloured into the palette; the brand sits around it.

Illustration

Gouache, the pinned house style: flat opaque colour, visible brush, paper showing through. A world topic keeps its own art (Hokusai's wave stays Hokusai's) inside Ochin's frame, palette and stitch.

Figures

Anything exact, such as a wavelength, a scale or a curve, is drawn as vectors to real data, never generated. Anything figurative may be painted. No invented portraits: without an honest photograph, a person gets initials in a disc.

Voice

কণ্ঠ

An elder sibling at the window: curious, warm, exact. Simple enough for a ten-year-old to follow, layered enough that an adult keeps finding more. It asks before it tells, names real people, and admits what nobody knows yet.

Ochin says

Niépce waited eight hours for a roof and a pear tree. Today your phone waits a thousandth of a second. What did we lose, and what did we gain?

Brand check

Prompts drift; code does not. Every page Ochin publishes passes these before anyone sees it.

  1. Every colour drawn is one of the ten.
  2. Every face is Galada, Tiro Bangla, Anek Bangla or Atma, at a ladder size.
  3. Text meets the contrast table above.
  4. Signature counts stay inside the rations.
  5. The section uses two section colours, no more.
  6. Folio present, same place, Bengali numerals.
  7. No text overlaps other text or a picture's subject; nothing overflows.
  8. Every photograph has a source and licence in the ledger.
